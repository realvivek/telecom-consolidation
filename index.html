United States telecommunications · 1983 to 2026

One company was broken into eight. Forty years later, the pieces had put themselves back into three.

In 1984 the Bell System was split apart to create competition. Competition's answer was to merge — 76 transactions over four decades, worth more than a trillion dollars. This page tracks 66 companies and shows, for each one, exactly which company it ended up inside.

      Where all 66 companies ended up

      Every company is one horizontal thread, named on the left. Time runs left to right. When a company was bought, its thread sweeps into the thread that bought it and stops there — so each block below is one surviving company and everything now inside it. Threads still independent today run all the way to the right edge and are drawn in blue; everything absorbed is grey.

        About the data

        Values are announced transaction values in nominal dollars, including assumed debt where that was the headline figure. A dash means the deal was a stock swap, a joint venture, or otherwise had no meaningful headline price. The set is curated rather than exhaustive: it covers the deals that moved the industry's structure, not every transaction ever filed.
